Ask any UK SME owner what their biggest software headache is, and you'll likely hear the same answer: "Our systems don't talk to each other."

A salesperson enters a customer's details in the CRM. The finance team re-enters the same details in Xero. The operations team enters them again in the job management system. When the customer changes their address, it gets updated in one system - maybe two - and the third one sends an invoice to the wrong place.

This is not a minor inconvenience. It is the single biggest hidden cost in most SMEs' technology stack. Duplicate data entry wastes hours of staff time every week. Inconsistent data between systems causes errors, delays, and customer complaints. And the problem gets worse as you add more tools - which most SMEs do, year after year.

## The Integration Problem

The average UK SME with 20–100 employees uses between 4 and 8 separate software systems. Each one was bought to solve a specific problem: a CRM for sales, Xero or Sage for accounting, a job management tool for operations, an e-commerce platform for online sales, maybe a inventory system, a project management tool, and a reporting dashboard.

Each tool is good at its job. The problem is the gaps between them. Data entered in one system doesn't appear in another. Reports need to be compiled manually from multiple sources. A customer's journey from lead to invoice to delivery touches four different systems, and none of them knows what the other is doing.

## Common Integration Scenarios

Most SME integration needs fall into a few recognisable patterns:

**CRM + Accounting:** When a deal is won in your CRM, the customer should be created in your accounting system automatically. Invoices raised in accounting should be visible in the CRM. This is the most common integration request - and the most common source of manual double-entry.

**E-commerce + Accounting + Inventory:** Online orders need to flow into your accounting system for invoicing and your inventory system for stock management. When stock levels change, the e-commerce platform needs to know so it doesn't sell something you don't have.

**Job Management + Invoicing:** When a job is completed, the details - hours worked, materials used, expenses incurred - should generate a draft invoice in your accounting system without anyone re-typing anything.

**CRM + Marketing Automation:** Customer behaviour and sales status should drive marketing communications automatically. A customer who has been in the pipeline for 60 days without movement should trigger a follow-up sequence without manual intervention.

## Three Approaches to Integration

There are three main ways to connect your systems, each with different trade-offs:

### 1. Custom API Integration

A developer writes code that connects your systems directly using their APIs. Data flows automatically - when a customer is created in your CRM, the code creates them in Xero within seconds.

**Pros:** Fast, reliable, exactly tailored to your workflow. No ongoing subscription costs beyond hosting. Full control over what syncs, when, and how.

**Cons:** Requires development upfront. Needs maintenance if any system changes its API. Best when you have a long-term relationship with a developer who can maintain it.

This is usually the most cost-effective option for SMEs with stable, well-defined integration needs. The build cost is typically £5,000–£20,000 depending on complexity, with minimal ongoing costs.

### 2. Middleware / iPaaS

Platforms like Zapier, Make (formerly Integromat), and Workato provide pre-built connectors between popular systems. You configure rules: "When a new customer is created in HubSpot, create them in Xero."

**Pros:** Quick to set up. No code required. Handles many common combinations out of the box.

**Cons:** Monthly subscription costs that scale with usage. Limited flexibility for complex logic. Can become expensive quickly - a busy Zapier account can cost £200–£500/month. Creates a dependency on a third-party platform.

Good for simple integrations between popular systems, but costs can spiral as you add more workflows and volume increases.

### 3. Custom Middleware Platform

A developer builds a lightweight middleware layer that sits between your systems and manages all integrations in one place. This is essentially building your own iPaaS, tailored to your specific needs.

**Pros:** Full control. No per-task pricing. Can handle complex logic and edge cases. Can be extended to new systems without starting from scratch.

**Cons:** Higher upfront cost. Requires development expertise to maintain.

Best for SMEs with 5+ systems to integrate or complex business logic that off-the-shelf middleware can't handle.

## What to Look for in an Integration Partner

If you're hiring someone to integrate your systems, here is what matters:

**API experience with your specific systems.** A developer who has integrated HubSpot with Xero before will do it faster and more reliably than one learning both APIs from scratch. Ask for specific examples.

**Understanding of your business process.** Integration is not just a technical problem - it's a business process problem. Your developer needs to understand how data flows through your business, not just how APIs work. The best integration partners ask about your workflow before they ask about your tech stack.

**Ongoing support plan.** APIs change. Systems update. Things break. Make sure your integration partner offers a maintenance arrangement - even if it's just a few hours per quarter to check everything is still working.

**Error handling and monitoring.** A good integration fails gracefully and tells you when something goes wrong. A bad one fails silently, and you discover the problem three weeks later when a customer complains. Ask how errors are handled and how you'll be notified.

**Data security and GDPR.** Customer data moving between systems must be handled securely and in compliance with GDPR. Your integration partner should be able to explain how data is transmitted, where it's stored, and what happens if a sync fails mid-way.

## Where to Start

Don't try to integrate everything at once. Start with the single most painful gap - the one that causes the most manual work or the most errors. For most SMEs, that's the CRM-to-accounting connection.

Get that working. Measure the time saved. Then tackle the next gap. Integration is iterative, and each successful connection builds confidence and demonstrates ROI.

The goal is not a perfectly integrated system. The goal is fewer spreadsheets, less double-entry, fewer errors, and more time spent on work that actually matters.
